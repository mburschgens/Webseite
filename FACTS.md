# FACTS.md — Königsforst

**Project:** Informational website about the Königsforst  
**Language:** German  
**Purpose:** Central collection of researched facts and content for the website.

This document is the content inventory and factual reference for the Königsforst website.

## 0. Content and verification rules

The information below is based on research collected for this project. A named source does not automatically mean that every detail has been independently verified.

Use the following status labels:

- **QUELLE ANGEGEBEN:** A source has been identified by the researcher.
- **ZU PRÜFEN:** The information needs another check before publication.
- **SAISONAL:** The information may change depending on the season.
- **UNBEKANNT:** No reliable information has been collected yet.

Rules for the website:
- Never invent missing distances, opening hours, prices, durations, or locations.
- Do not present unconfirmed information as fact.
- Keep source references attached to the information they support.
- For seasonal attractions and wildlife, explain that sightings are not guaranteed.
- For current information, record the last verification date.
- Do not treat individual forest paths as complete circular routes unless a source explicitly describes them as such.

# 4. Attractions and destinations (Sehenswürdigkeiten)

The following places can be displayed as individual points of interest (POIs) on the website.

## 4.1 Monte Troodelöh

| Information | Details |
|---|---|
| Name | Monte Troodelöh |
| Elevation | 118.04 m |
| Location | Along the Wolfsweg |
| Special feature | Highest geographical point of Cologne |

Monte Troodelöh is the highest geographical point in Cologne. It is located in the middle of the forest.

Visitors should not expect a large mountain or panoramic viewpoint. It is a gentle elevation in dense woodland, marked by a large erratic boulder and a wooden cross with a summit logbook.

**Sources:**
- Stadt Köln / regional information
- Kölner Stadt-Anzeiger – Radtour durch den Königsforst
- Additional local references collected during research

**Status:** QUELLE ANGEGEBEN

## 4.8 Kaisereiche

A historic crossing point in the forest.

The original oak was planted in 1908 in honour of Kaiser Wilhelm II. The original tree was later replaced, but the location remains a notable point along several walking routes.

**Source:** Local research references collected for the project.

**Status:** ZU PRÜFEN – historical details and exact location.

## 4.9 Alte Bahntrasse Brück–Forsbach

A former railway route that crossed the Königsforst.

Parts of the former railway corridor can now be used as straight, partly paved paths. Old railway embankments or foundations may still be visible.

**Source:** Local research references collected for the project.

# 5. Flora and fauna (Pflanzen und Tiere)

The Königsforst contains different forest habitats, including old beech and oak woodland, wet areas, streams, and open spaces.

The following species were collected during research. Their presence, conservation status, and exact distribution should be verified before publication.

## 5.1 Wildlife (Fauna)

### Birds

The Königsforst is described as an important bird habitat. The collected research mentions approximately 60 breeding bird species and a notable density of woodpeckers.

| Species | German name | Interesting information |
|---|---|---|
| Black woodpecker | Schwarzspecht | Europe's largest woodpecker. Creates large nesting cavities in old trees, which can later be used by other species. |
| Middle spotted woodpecker | Mittelspecht | Depends on old oak and beech woodland with deadwood. |
| Grey-headed woodpecker | Grauspecht | Associated with older woodland and deadwood habitats. |
| European honey buzzard | Wespenbussard | Feeds mainly on wasp larvae. |
| Common kingfisher | Eisvogel | Hunts near natural streams and watercourses. |
| Wood warbler | Waldlaubsänger | Small woodland bird. |
| Tree pipit | Baumpieper | Bird associated with open woodland and forest edges. |

### Important research observation

The information about the Königsforst is currently spread across different websites, social media profiles, maps, information boards, and individual providers.

The new website should bring relevant information together in one accessible place, especially:
- Route information and starting points
- Family-friendly destinations
- Flora and fauna
- Activities and guided tours
- Visitor rules and safety
- Practical visitor information

The goal is not to replace official sources, but to make their information easier to find and understand.

# 12. Content principles for the website

1. The website should help visitors plan a visit before arriving and find useful information while already in the forest.
2. Families should be able to identify short, interesting routes with attractions along the way.
3. Nature information should make flora and fauna interesting and understandable without promising animal sightings.
4. Route descriptions should include distance, estimated duration, effort, starting point, and attractions wherever reliable data exists.
5. Cycling, walking, and horse-riding routes must be clearly distinguished.
6. Individual forest paths must not be confused with complete routes.
7. Activities outside the forest should be labelled as nearby rather than inside the Königsforst.
8. Current information must include a source and verification date whenever possible.
9. Missing or unconfirmed information must be communicated honestly.
10. Information should be accessible without requiring visitors to use social media.
