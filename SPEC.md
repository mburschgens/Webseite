# SPEC.md --- Königsforst Website

## 1. Project Overview

**Project:** Informational website for the Königsforst\
**Primary goal:** Help visitors plan and enjoy a visit to the
Königsforst by making practical information, routes, attractions, nature
information, and visitor guidance easy to find.\
**Target audiences:** Families with children, walkers, cyclists, dog
owners, nature-interested visitors, returning visitors, and people who
need accessible route information.

The website should be informative, approachable, visually engaging, and
useful both before a visit and while visitors are already in the forest.
Its structure is inspired by the editorial, lively presentation of *Mit
Vergnügen* websites, while remaining clear and accessible.

## 2. Gap Statement

An older couple is looking for a quick stop to grab a bite to eat on
their way home from a trip through the Königsforst. They want to know
whether Schmitzebud is still open and what food is available. Since they
are on a budget, they also want to check the prices and whether card
payments are accepted. However, they cannot find a website that answers
these questions. The restaurant only has a basic Facebook profile with
limited information, not much about opening hours, the menu, the
atmosphere, and upcoming events. Some additional information is
available on Instagram, which they do not use.

**Website implication:** Relevant visitor information should be findable
without requiring visitors to use social media. Nearby food and
facilities (including opening hours, menu/prices, payment options, and
events where verified) should be included or linked from a clearly
labeled visitor-information section. Information that has not been
verified must not be presented as fact.

## 3. Main Situations / User Needs

### Situation 1 --- A mother visiting with her 10-year-old daughter

A young mother is exploring the Königsforst with her 10-year-old
daughter. They want an easy, family-friendly walking route with
interesting things to discover along the way, so the child does not get
bored.

User quote: \> "Also es wäre schon praktisch gewesen, wenn ich gewusst
hätte wie weit wir bis zum Matschloch noch gehen... Sie hat schnell
keine Lust mehr wenn wir die ganze Zeit nur am gehen sind... dann hätte
ich auch einen anderen Eingang oder so genommen. Außerdem gibt es
Kaulquappen, als meine Tochter extra wegen denen dahin wollte waren
leider alle schon weg."

**Needs:** - Route length, estimated duration, difficulty, and starting
point / entrance. - Family-friendly routes and attractions, with
distances between destinations. - Information about what children can
discover, such as seasonal nature observations. - Clear indication when
an attraction or nature feature is seasonal or may not currently be
present. - Picnic / pause places and practical planning information.

### Situation 2 --- A returning visitor interested in nature and family activities

A man who used to live near the forest has moved away. He visits a
friend who still lives nearby, and they go for a long walk. He knows the
paths well and previously visited the forest with his family so the
children could play. He is especially interested in the flora and fauna
and feels that opportunities for children and family outings are not
represented very well.

**Needs:** - Information about local plants and animals. - Nature
discovery content and notable places. - A more visible overview of
family activities and destinations. - Useful information for both
experienced visitors and people planning a family trip.

## 6. Homepage --- First Screen (Above the Fold)

The first screen should immediately communicate what the website is
about and provide useful entry points for planning a visit.

### Required elements

1.  **Forest map**
    -   A map overview of the Königsforst.
    -   Show hiking / walking paths, cycling paths, riding paths, and
        attractions where reliable data is available.
    -   Use a clear legend and distinguish route types visually.
    -   Map controls must be usable with keyboard and touch; provide an
        equivalent text-based route / attraction list.
2.  **Two attraction photographs side by side**
    -   Two engaging photos of notable attractions or places in the
        forest.
    -   Each photo should have a short, descriptive title and concise
        information.
    -   The photos should link to more detailed attraction information.
3.  **Short introductory text**
    -   Briefly introduce the Königsforst and what visitors can do
        there.
    -   Mention a selection of attractions, nature discoveries, routes,
        and family-friendly possibilities.
    -   Keep the text concise and scannable.
4.  **Clear navigation / entry points**
    -   Homepage sections or navigation links for: "About Your Visit,"
        "Activities," "Forest Routes," and "All About Nature."
    -   News / current notices and general rules should be easy to
        locate.

### Visual direction

-   Take inspiration from the editorial and lively structure of *Mit
    Vergnügen* websites.
-   Use a clear hierarchy, expressive imagery, and short blocks of text.
-   Keep the interface uncluttered; visual character must not compromise
    readability or accessibility.

## 7. General Rules and Accessibility

The website must be designed inclusively and aim to meet **WCAG 2.2
Level AA** as a practical accessibility target, supporting the
accessibility requirements relevant under the **European Accessibility
Act (EAA)**. The exact legal applicability of the EAA depends on the
service and provider; this specification treats accessibility as a core
design and implementation requirement.

Requirements: - Semantic HTML structure and meaningful heading order. -
Full keyboard navigation; visible focus indicators. - Text alternatives
for meaningful images; decorative images use empty alt text. -
Sufficient color contrast (at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for
large text and essential UI graphics, according to WCAG guidance). - Do
not rely on color alone to communicate route types, status, or
meaning. - Responsive layouts and controls that work with touch,
keyboard, and assistive technology. - Accessible names and labels for
controls, map controls, and links. - Clear, plain language; avoid
unnecessarily complex wording. - Respect reduced-motion preferences and
avoid essential information conveyed only through animation. -
Accessible alternatives to interactive maps, including route
descriptions and lists. - Images and content should not obscure text or
cause loss of information when zoomed. - Forms (if any) must have
labels, helpful error messages, and accessible feedback. - Identify
current, seasonal, or uncertain information clearly. Do not fabricate
opening hours, route conditions, wildlife sightings, or facility
details.

## 8. Acceptance Criteria

The website will be checked against the following criteria:

1.  **Responsive layout --- smartphone:** At a viewport width of
    approximately **360 px**, the page has no horizontal scrolling or
    clipped content. Navigation, text, map alternatives, and attraction
    cards remain usable.

2.  **Responsive layout --- laptop / small monitor:** At viewport widths
    of **1366 px to 1440 px**, the homepage displays the map, the two
    attraction cards, introductory text, and navigation in a balanced
    layout with clear visual hierarchy.

3.  **First-screen content:** On the homepage, the first screen includes
    a forest map (or a clearly represented map area), two attraction
    photographs side by side, and a short introduction that names or
    previews forest attractions and activities.

4.  **Information architecture and question coverage:** Each of the
    listed questions Q01--Q19 is assigned to a clearly labeled website
    area according to the tree and priority table. Visitors can reach
    the relevant answer through navigation or contextual links.

5.  **Accessibility / EAA-oriented check:** Core pages and interactive
    elements can be navigated using only a keyboard; focus is visible;
    text and interface contrast meet WCAG 2.2 AA targets; images have
    appropriate alt text; and the map has a usable text-based
    alternative. Check at 200% zoom and with a screen reader on key page
    flows.

## 9. Content and Data Quality

-   Prioritize information that helps visitors decide where to enter,
    which route to take, what to expect, and how to behave safely.
-   Route details should include distance, estimated duration,
    difficulty, route type, starting point, and accessibility notes
    wherever available.
-   Seasonal attractions (e.g. tadpoles) should include a seasonal note
    rather than promising that they will always be present.
-   Visitor facilities such as toilets, picnic places, restaurants, and
    Schmitzebud should only show details that have been verified.
    Include a last-checked date for time-sensitive details when
    possible.
-   Provide updates / notices for temporary closures or safety-related
    restrictions when authoritative information is available.
-   Use consistent labels and terminology across navigation, map legend,
    and content pages.
